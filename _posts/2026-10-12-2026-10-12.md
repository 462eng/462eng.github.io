The day started on the network map and ended in Home Assistant, with a
power cut in the middle that explained two surprises. Details are in
the linked posts.

## Network map: Wi-Fi, Zigbee, moved cables

Our inventory tool now draws Wi-Fi and Zigbee next to the cables, and it
recognises a machine replugged on another switch port (one alert and a
one-click fix instead of three alerts). The Wi-Fi collector has to deal
with an old access point that ejects your admin session. The Zigbee
collector talks to Home Assistant's websocket from plain PHP. After the
power cut, a storage-network switch came back with its old address:
its settings had never been saved.
→ [Network map: Wi-Fi, Zigbee, moved cables]({% post_url 2026-10-12-network-map-wifi-zigbee-moved-cables %})

## The AC stayed off, and a loop with no hidden state

The air conditioner, used here as a fan, had stayed off since the
afternoon. No automation had done it: a power cut had restarted Home
Assistant, and the smart plug's power-on state was set to `Off`. We then
made it start on high CO2 or VOC. The first version kept a flag
remembering who had switched it on. It was replaced by helpers
recomputed every minute (one per pollutant, each with its own
hysteresis), which the plug follows.
→ [A stateless air-circulation loop in Home Assistant]({% post_url 2026-10-12-stateless-air-circulation-home-assistant %})

## Heating load from a power meter

Total power is also the heat produced indoors. From it we got the
convector's real power (980 W), the room's heat loss (about 105 W/K)
and its base load (about 620 W). This gives a heating-load percentage,
measured and forecast from the weather service, relative to the
convectors that are actually online (one is broken). The dashboard
icons turn green, yellow, red or purple depending on whether the
setpoint can be held. There is also a card-mod 4.2 gotcha with glance
cards.
→ [Heating load from a power meter, with a forecast]({% post_url 2026-10-12-heating-load-from-a-power-meter %})

## How it connects

1. Airing the room to clear CO2 and VOC means bringing in outdoor air,
   and in autumn that **adds heating load**.
2. Knowing the heating load is how a future fan, which will also open a
   ventilation damper, can decide whether airing the room is cheap or
   expensive right now.
3. Both use the same pattern: **helpers recomputed from sensors on a
   schedule**, and devices that follow them.
4. The **power cut** explains two surprises: the storage switch that
   forgot its settings, and the AC plug that came back off.
5. The **Zigbee view** of the network map showed the plug of the broken
   convector offline since late September. The power meter told the
   same story: in a week of data, the second convector never ran.
