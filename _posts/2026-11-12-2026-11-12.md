Two unrelated things today: a new tool of our own, and an old switch
taken apart. Details are in the linked posts.

## A HAWK for mechanical CAD

After HAWK for electronics, the same idea for mechanical design. A part
is OpenSCAD code; our own interpreter evaluates it (checked against the
real OpenSCAD, 122 renders out of 122 matching) and a kernel on
OpenCascade builds an exact B-rep, so a cylinder exports as a true
cylinder in STEP, not facets. Claude writes the code and decides the
part; the tool computes the geometry, the assemblies, the kinematics
and the drawings. First real test: a humanoid robot arm of about fifty
parts.
→ [A HAWK for mechanical CAD]({% post_url 2026-11-12-mechanical-cad-openscad-exact-step %})

## A five-port switch, taken apart

A NETGEAR GS105E firmware, from the factory file down: no OS, a Dallas
8051 in flat 24-bit mode, a Broadcom switch. The web interface it
seems to carry does not work — the port just echoes back whatever you
send — and the HTML and images it stores belong to a D-Link, embedded
whole and never removed. The one reusable result, the NSDP management
protocol, is already out as a command-line tool.
→ [A five-port switch, taken apart]({% post_url 2026-11-12-gs105e-switch-firmware-echo-web-server %})
